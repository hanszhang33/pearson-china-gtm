2.2 The enterprise language-upskilling journey (the customer's job-to-be-done)

Any company trying to lift its people's English job-capability — for overseas posts, cross-border teams, or customer-facing roles — goes through the same recurring loop. This is vendor-neutral; it is the work itself:

1. Diagnose — Where is the person today vs. what the role demands?

2. Benchmark — Set the target on a common ruler (a role-based English standard).

3. Learn — Build the missing skill.

4. Practice in the flow of work — Use it on the job, not just in a classroom.

5. Verify / certify — Prove the gain; issue a credential.

6. Measure & loop — Did capability move? Did the business move? Then re-diagnose.

The vendor who can stand beside the customer at every step — on one consistent ruler — is the vendor who owns the relationship.

Each stage of that journey maps to a Pearson asset, and every asset speaks the same language: the Global Scale of English (GSE).

5.3 Key correction: don't sponsor "academic conferences"

You mentioned sponsoring academic conferences — that's the classic newcomer trap. Academic conferences (professors, researchers, universities) are NOT enterprise buyers; you won't collect CHRO cards there. Enterprises attend HR / human-capital / training / outbound / AI+learning summits. The former builds academic reputation; the latter converts to meetings and pipeline. Keep Pearson's academic relationships (PTE / English Connect) for brand backing; run enterprise acquisition through industry summits.

5.4 The 30-day contact-building cadence

1. Named decision-makers — use Maimai / Qichacha / LinkedIn to put a real name on each of the 50 accounts.

2. A/B/C tiering — by outbound intensity + headcount.

3. Warm path first — ask Mark, China CEO, Microsoft AM for introductions before any cold touch.

4. Join associations — pick 2-3 sector chambers for partner status.

5. Maimai cadence — add 5 target decision-makers and post 1 value nugget daily.

6. Hook to break ice — free Versant pilot for one hiring cycle.

5.5 The hook: break ice with a free Versant pilot

Don't lead with an annual contract. Use "free / low-cost Versant pilot for one hiring cycle (200-500 tests) → GSE baseline report to CHRO / overseas GM" as the ice-breaker. The report itself becomes the opener for the next Mondly expansion (see §4.2 diagnostic hook). This turns "cold outreach" into "they ask you to interpret the report."

5.6 Relationship to the 3.3 channel model

§3.3 is the framework (direct lighthouse + co-sell scale); this chapter turns "scale" into nine executable reach channels. Framework says mode; this chapter says motion.

One-line strategy: P0 trio (associations + ecosystem co-sell + ABM warm intros) as the base; P1 summits + Maimai + content for volume; P2 cold + alumni as supplement. You are not blindly cold-calling alone — the 50-account list, the diagnostic weapon, and Group backing are already in hand; what's missing is the execution discipline to build contact, one account at a time.
