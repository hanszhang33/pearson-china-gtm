1. Why China, Why Now

The Chinese outbound wave has moved from "product export" to "capability export," and language is the bottleneck McKinsey finds >50% of firms call their toughest internationalisation challenge — with 78% reporting a "talent fault line" going global (Neeley, HBS).

The going-global story is now "Outbound 3.0" — not just shipping goods but operating capabilities abroad. That shift is precisely what turns language from a soft skill into a hard operational constraint, and it is the opening our Enterprise Skilling play is built for.

2.2 The full journey of lifting English job-capability (the customer's "job to be done")

Any company trying to lift its people's English job-capability — for overseas posts, cross-border teams, or customer-facing roles — goes through the same recurring loop. This is vendor-neutral; it is the work itself. The vendor who can stand beside the customer at every step, on one ruler, owns the relationship.

1. Diagnose — where is the person today vs. what the role demands?

2. Benchmark — set the target on a common ruler (a role-based English standard).

3. Learn — build the missing skill.

4. Practice in the flow of work — use it on the job, not just in a classroom.

5. Verify / certify — prove the gain; issue a credential.

6. Measure & loop — did capability move? Did the business move? Then re-diagnose.

2.3 How Pearson powers every step

Each stage of that journey maps to a Pearson product, and every product speaks the same language: the Global Scale of English (GSE).

5.3 Key correction: don't sponsor "academic conferences"

Academic conferences (professors, researchers, universities) are NOT enterprise buyers — sponsoring them won't collect CHRO cards; enterprises attend HR / human-capital / training / outbound / AI+learning summits. The former builds academic reputation; the latter converts to meetings and pipeline. Keep Pearson's academic relationships (PTE / English Connect) for brand backing; run enterprise acquisition through industry summits.
